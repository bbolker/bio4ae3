## essay questions, final test

Three of these questions will be on the final test; you'll choose two.

1. How do "Red Queen" coevolutionary dynamics differ from "trench warfare" coevolution?  Define each term and explain the kinds of genes and traits that we would expect to be involved in each type of coevolution. Describe one experiment we might do or set of observations we might make that would determine which type of coevolution was occurring in a particular host-parasite system.

2. Define host resistance and tolerance to parasites; draw a diagram, properly labeled, to illustrate the difference between them. Describe the evolutionary processes that tend to drive resistance alleles toward polymorphism and tolerance alleles toward fixation within a population (i.e., either absent or [close to] 100%).

3. Name three characteristics of parasites that affect the probability that a new resistance mutation will appear (*de novo* mutation), and three characteristics that affect the probability that one will spread in the population once it occurs (evolution based on *standing variation*). Using these characteristics, explain why *de novo* mutation is characteristic of viral resistance evolution and evolution based on standing variation is characteristic of bacterial resistance evolution.

4. Given a host trait that changes when the host is infected (e.g. level of activity, attraction to feline urine, etc.), describe a general framework for determining whether the change constitutes (a) host manipulation by the parasite; (b) host resistance; (c) host tolerance; (d) host adaption to increase its inclusive fitness; (e) a pathological response by the host. (You can use a table to present a compact description, but you should also give a brief explanation justifying each inference on logical grounds.

5. Explain why we would normally expect a 50/50 sex ratio in obligately sexually reproducing, dioecious/gonchoric organisms. How does the presence of vertically (maternally) transmitted parasites affect this expectation? Why?

6. Explain how the virulence tradeoff hypothesis might apply to HIV. For this parasite, what are useful definitions of virulence, transmission rate, and parasite load? What observational evidence do we have that the relationships among these characteristics would select for an intermediate optimum of virulence? Finally, what evidence do we have for the actual change over time in HIV virulence in human populations?

7. Explain two or three general ways that we can provide empirical support for whether a particular change in host traits upon infection constitutes an example of parasite manipulation. For each, describe the logic and specify whether it provides a direct test of manipulation or just general support for the manipulation hypothesis. Give examples from actual studies of real host-parasite systems for at least two types of support.
